Oral Minoxidil for Hair Loss: What a Review Found

Key takeaways

  • Minoxidil was first developed as a blood-pressure medication; topical minoxidil is a well-known hair-loss treatment.
  • A 2021 review examined low-dose oral minoxidil for several types of hair loss.
  • The review reports encouraging results but relies largely on smaller and retrospective studies, and it lists side effects.

What it is

Minoxidil widens blood vessels and has a long history in hair-loss treatment. In recent years, clinicians have used low doses taken by mouth as an alternative for some patients.

What the evidence says

The review summarizes studies of low-dose oral minoxidil in androgenetic alopecia and other hair-loss conditions and reports that many patients showed improvement. The most common side effect described is excess hair growth elsewhere on the body. Less common effects relate to the medication's blood-pressure activity, such as fluid retention and changes in heart rate.

What the evidence does not establish

The review notes that much of the evidence comes from retrospective and small studies rather than large randomized trials. It does not establish the best dose for every person, and it does not suggest this is a do-it-yourself treatment.

Who may want to pay attention

Anyone with a heart or blood-pressure condition, or who is pregnant or breastfeeding, should speak to a clinician before considering any form of minoxidil. Hair-loss treatment is best guided by a proper diagnosis.

Sources

  1. Randolph M, Tosti A. Oral minoxidil treatment for hair loss: a review of efficacy and safety. J Am Acad Dermatol. 2021;84(3):737–746. View on PubMed
